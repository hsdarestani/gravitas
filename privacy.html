2. Principles, purposes and legal bases

We process personal data only where there is a lawful basis. Depending on the activity, the bases are Article 6(1)(b) GDPR (providing requested account and platform services), Article 6(1)(a) (consent, such as newsletter subscription and non-essential storage), Article 6(1)(f) (legitimate interests in secure, reliable and abuse-resistant operations and in moderating public contributions), and Article 6(1)(c) (legal obligations). Legitimate interests are balanced against the rights and interests of affected individuals.

You can browse public content without creating an account. An email address and suitable sign-in credentials are needed to register; other information is requested only where relevant to a chosen feature. If required account information is not supplied, account features cannot be provided. Optional profile details, research submissions, integrations and newsletter subscriptions are voluntary.

3. Hosting, connection data and security

The site's configured infrastructure uses STRATO hosting and email services and a server-side application backed by PostgreSQL. On access, the hosting environment and security systems may process IP address, request time, requested URL, browser/user-agent and referrer data, response status, diagnostic information and records of security-related activity. This is necessary to deliver pages, troubleshoot incidents and protect against misuse (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). Operators and technical processors may access data only as required for these purposes.

Security and diagnostic records are retained for a period proportionate to the relevant operational or security purpose, subject to incident investigations and legal preservation requirements. Exact server-log and backup rotation periods depend on the deployed infrastructure and must be confirmed against live operational settings; this notice does not promise a fixed period that the running service has not verified.

4. Account registration, login and profile

For an account, we process data such as name or display name, email address, password hash if you use email/password registration, verification and login status, profile fields, role and access permissions, account timestamps and session information (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR). Passwords for password-based accounts are stored as hashes rather than in plaintext. Additional profile details, such as an avatar, affiliation or research interests, may be stored when supplied.

If you choose Continue with Google, you are redirected to Google's sign-in service. The integration uses the information returned by Google to authenticate you and create or match a GraviQuest account; the code handles an email address, email-verification state and name. The use of Google's services is also subject to Google's own privacy information. GraviQuest does not receive your Google password.

The platform uses authenticated sessions and permissions to separate public, member, research and internal-team functionality. Administrators or authorised team members may be able to access profile and account information where needed to provide support, administer access or investigate abuse.

5. Newsletter and transactional emails

If you subscribe to the newsletter, we store your email address, subscription source and confirmation/activation state, along with related timestamps. Subscription is subject to a confirmation step (double opt-in). Newsletter processing is based on consent (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR); you can withdraw consent and unsubscribe at any time. System messages needed for account verification, password recovery, access or security are separate from marketing consent and are processed as necessary to provide the service (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR and, where applicable, Article 6(1)(f)). Email delivery is configured through STRATO SMTP.

6. Community, support and published contributions

For discussions, comments, reactions, community profiles, support requests and moderation, we process the content you submit, associated account/display name, links and attachments where provided, moderation or ticket status and timestamps (Article 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR). Public posts and their display names may be visible to other visitors and can remain referenced in discussions. Private support communications are intended for authorised support personnel. Please do not post confidential or sensitive personal information in public areas.

7. Research projects, workspaces, files and collaboration

The Research, Core and related workspaces may store project names and descriptions, uploaded documents and datasets, pages, notes, tasks, schedules, collaboration records, comments, memberships, sharing links, access decisions, activity history and associated metadata. Files and project resources may be stored or synchronised in the managed Nextcloud environment. Access depends on workspace, project and document permissions; invited collaborators and authorised administrators may access material within their assigned roles (Article 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR).

People uploading documents are responsible for ensuring that they have appropriate rights and a lawful basis to share any personal data in them. Academic or institutional users must not assume that uploading participant, patient or special-category research data is permitted merely because a workspace is private. Additional agreements, security measures or consent or other lawful conditions may be needed. Public sharing links may make the content accessible to anyone who receives the link, according to its configured permissions.

8. Learning, labs and research activity

Learning and laboratory features may record course enrolments, completion and assessment states, interactive simulation parameters, experiment snapshots, scores, learning progress, comments and timestamps. When enabled, the platform may connect to Open edX and linked learning tools for these purposes (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR). The precise categories depend on the features used and whether optional learning integrations are enabled.

9. Pulsar and other AI-assisted features

When you use Pulsar or AI-assisted research tools, the service may process your prompts, generated answers, relevant workspace content you are authorised to access, selected sources, conversation context, provider/model information, usage and error metadata. For signed-in users, the application can store conversation runs, input and output text, thread state and snapshots, and saved memory entries or preferences in order to provide continuity and requested functions (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR and, for integrity, security and misuse prevention, Article 6(1)(f)). Depending on the feature, a public-visitor request may be processed without a registered account.

AI requests may be transmitted to an external model provider selected by the service or by the user. The code supports Cloudflare Workers AI and, where configured, OpenAI, Anthropic and Google Gemini, as well as user-configured OpenAI-compatible providers. This list describes supported integrations, not a guarantee that all are enabled in production. Which service actually receives a request depends on the active configuration, the tool selected and possible fallback routing. A provider may process the submitted prompt and contextual material in accordance with the applicable service contract and privacy terms.

If you configure your own AI provider, the platform stores the provider details and an encrypted API key to enable your selected connection. Content submitted to that provider is governed by that provider's terms, privacy arrangements and processing location. We do not represent that every external model is located in the EEA, or that all providers follow the same retention or model-training policy. Do not enter special-category or highly confidential personal data into AI features unless you have confirmed that the specific processing arrangement is appropriate.

AI-generated content can be inaccurate. AI features support research and learning, and are not intended to make decisions that produce legal or similarly significant effects about individuals solely by automated means.

10. Connected third-party services and optional integrations

Depending on what you choose to use, integrations may connect to Google sign-in or Google Calendar, Nextcloud file storage, Open edX learning services, external data and research sources, or optional messaging and notification services. Only information needed for the requested integration should be exchanged, such as authorisation tokens, account identifiers, event metadata, selected documents or user-supplied search terms. Third-party services can be independent controllers or processors depending on the arrangement; their own privacy information may also apply. A service being present in the code does not mean every user is connected to it.

11. Cookies, local storage and consent

Technically necessary cookies and similar storage support login, session integrity, CSRF protection, security, and features expressly requested by users. The site also uses local storage for interface settings such as visual theme, reading preferences and privacy choices. Their legal basis is Article 6(1)(b) or (f) GDPR as relevant; access to or storage on the device is assessed under Section 25 TDDDG, including its necessity exception.

Optional analytics, advertising or other non-essential device storage, if introduced, must not be activated before valid consent (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR and Section 25(1) TDDDG). When optional analytics is offered, the consent interface provides an option to reject it and to change or withdraw a prior choice. No advertising or tracking use should be inferred merely from the existence of the consent code. Local storage may remain on your device until cleared through your browser or the relevant preference controls.

12. Data recipients and international transfers

Recipients may include hosting, infrastructure and mail providers, authorised collaborators, and providers of services that you explicitly enable or invoke (including Google authentication and the selected AI provider). Access is limited to the scope necessary for the relevant function. Where a vendor processes personal data on our behalf, an appropriate Article 28 GDPR arrangement must be in place where legally required.

When personal data is transferred outside the European Economic Area, the applicable transfer mechanism must be assessed for the actual recipient and processing arrangement. Depending on the recipient, this may involve a European Commission adequacy decision or appropriate safeguards such as the EU Standard Contractual Clauses and any necessary supplementary measures. To request details of the safeguards applicable to your processing, contact us at the address above. We do not assume that every integration has the same transfer mechanism.

13. Retention, deletion and backups

We keep personal data only for as long as necessary for the stated purposes and any applicable legal or legitimate preservation requirements. Account and profile information is ordinarily needed while the account is active; newsletter information is maintained while the subscription remains valid and for the time needed to document consent or withdrawal; research and collaborative records may have a different lifecycle because they belong to shared projects; security records are held for operational and incident-response purposes. AI conversations, memory records, attached files and snapshots may remain associated with an account or project until deleted through the available workflow or a valid request, subject to lawful restrictions.

Account deletion is supported in the application but may be unavailable for accounts using a sign-in method without a local password, or where deletion would conflict with protected collaborative records. The self-service account data export may not include all research files or AI history. If either tool does not meet your needs, contact us to request access, export, correction, deletion or restriction; requests are assessed under the GDPR, and we will explain any lawful retention or technical limitations. Backup copies can persist until their scheduled rotation, and recovery processes must respect applicable deletion obligations.

14. Security

We use access permissions, transport security, password hashing, protected session cookies, encryption for stored AI provider credentials and other technical and organisational safeguards appropriate to the service. No internet service can promise absolute security. Users should protect their credentials and manage project membership and sharing links carefully.

15. Your rights and complaints

Subject to the conditions of the GDPR, you may request access (Article 15), rectification (Article 16), erasure (Article 17), restriction (Article 18), data portability (Article 20), and object to relevant processing (Article 21). Where we rely on consent, you may withdraw it at any time without affecting the lawfulness of processing before withdrawal. You may also lodge a complaint with a competent data-protection supervisory authority, including an authority in the EU country of your habitual residence, place of work or the alleged infringement. Requests can be sent to the controller contact above; we may need to verify your identity before disclosing personal data.

16. Children and sensitive data

The service is designed for general science, learning and research activity, not for collecting sensitive information about children or special-category personal data by default. If a research project involves health data, biometric data, other Article 9 GDPR categories or participants who require additional protection, the responsible research institution or project owner must determine and document the appropriate lawful conditions before any upload or AI processing.

17. Changes to this notice

We may update this notice when the service, data flows, providers or legal requirements change. A revised date will appear at the top of this page. For material changes, we will take further notice or consent steps where required by law.
