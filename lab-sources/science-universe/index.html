EDITING WORLD A
RESEARCH SIMULATION / SYNTHETIC DATANOT A FORECAST · NOT A SCIENTIFIC CONFIDENCE SCORE

Read the model before reading the result.

Scientific statistical core, synthetic ecosystem. Each hypothesis has an assumed hidden truth and an effect size. The engine now samples Gaussian evidence using a known-variance, two-sided z test: Z = √n · θ + ε, with ε ~ N(0,1), and tests p ≤ α. Independent replication draws fresh evidence. These are established statistical rules, not an ad hoc probability-of-success curve.

Publication pressure can trigger additional uncorrected analyses as a simplified demonstration of selection bias. Review capacity, false-claim priors, per-discipline base rates, lab budgets, agent behavior, retraction thresholds and citation effects remain unvalidated model assumptions and must not be presented as measured facts.

Scientific foundations and provenance:

  • Ioannidis (2005): prior odds, statistical power, false positive findings ↗Conceptual evidence for linking pre-study odds and positive predictive value; our base rates are not taken from this paper.
  • Simmons, Nelson & Simonsohn (2011): analysis flexibility and false positives ↗Motivates the multiple-analyses mechanism. The number of analyses in this model is assumed.
  • Smaldino & McElreath (2016): incentives and research methodology ↗Motivates research incentive dynamics; does not estimate our policy coefficients.
  • GraviQuest model specification, assumptions and limitations ↗Exact implemented rules and explicit separation of calibrated vs assumed parameters.

Parallel worlds share a saved state but are not real-world causal estimates. AI agents are rule-driven simulation actors, not separate generative LLM researchers. The current peer review rule uses only observable method and preregistration evidence; its rejection probabilities are still hypotheses, not calibrated reviewer measurements.
