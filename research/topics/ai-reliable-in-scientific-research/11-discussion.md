# Discussion

## Main question

**If an AI produces a result that no human can explain, but the result survives every test we can run, what exactly have we learned?**

## Prompts

1. Would you cite an AI-generated literature synthesis if every cited paper were real and every citation were manually checked?
2. Is a machine-generated proof accepted by Lean more trustworthy than a human proof understood by only a few specialists?
3. Should scientific AI systems receive benchmark credit for refusing to answer?
4. When should a mechanistic model be preferred even if a black-box predictor is more accurate?
5. Is a hypothesis scientifically valuable before anyone is willing or able to test it?
6. If AI increases individual productivity while narrowing the collective research space, is science progressing?
7. Should journals require disclosure of all generative AI use, or only use that materially affects scientific content?
8. Is independent replication still independent if both teams use the same foundation model?
9. What should happen when a model is right but its cited rationale is wrong?
10. Which parts of the scientific method are easiest to automate safely, and which are the hardest?

## Discussion quality rule

Prefer concrete cases and falsifiable arguments.

Avoid:
- “AI is just a tool” without specifying the workflow;
- “AI will replace scientists” without a scientific task;
- “humans also make mistakes” as a complete answer;
- generic optimism or pessimism disconnected from verification.
