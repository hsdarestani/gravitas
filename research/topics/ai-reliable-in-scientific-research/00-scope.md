# Scope

## Primary question

**Is AI reliable in scientific research?**

This is too broad to answer as a yes/no question. The research therefore decomposes “AI reliability” by scientific task and by verification regime.

## Stronger formulation

**Under what conditions should an AI-assisted scientific output be treated as evidence rather than merely as a candidate?**

## Subquestions

1. What kinds of AI output are already scientifically useful?
2. Which outputs are easiest and hardest to verify?
3. Why do hallucinations remain relevant even as models improve?
4. How do evaluation incentives affect hallucination and abstention?
5. What is “AI slop” in a scientific context, beyond bad prose?
6. What safeguards are journals, repositories and research communities using?
7. Does a correct prediction count as scientific understanding?
8. What is the difference between prediction, simulation and mechanism?
9. What changes when AI generates hypotheses rather than only analyses data?
10. What changes when verification itself is machine-executable?
11. Can AI improve individual productivity while harming science as a collective search process?
12. What should be logged, disclosed and reproducible in an AI-assisted research workflow?

## In scope

- LLMs in research synthesis and scholarly writing;
- specialized scientific ML systems;
- AI-supported hypothesis generation;
- autonomous or multi-agent research systems;
- formal verification in mathematics;
- computational reproducibility;
- publication integrity;
- hallucinated references;
- verification bottlenecks;
- uncertainty and abstention;
- epistemic monoculture and topic narrowing.

## Out of scope for this Topic

- general AI existential-risk debate;
- broad labor-market effects of AI;
- generic AI ethics not specific to research reliability;
- exhaustive regulation by country;
- clinical deployment safety except where it illustrates scientific verification;
- “consciousness” or whether AI literally understands.

## Core distinction

The Topic must consistently distinguish:

**Generator quality** from **workflow reliability**.

A generator can be fallible while the final artifact is strongly verified. Conversely, a fluent generator can be impressive while the overall workflow is scientifically weak.

## Research stance

The research should resist two easy narratives:

- “AI hallucinates, therefore it cannot do science.”
- “AI has solved major scientific problems, therefore it is trustworthy.”

Both erase the structure that actually matters: task, evidence, verifier, uncertainty, distribution shift and accountability.
