# Contradiction and challenge layer

This file exists to prevent the research pack from becoming a thesis-confirmation machine.

## Challenge 1 — A model does not need mechanistic understanding to contribute to science

The strongest objection to an explanation-heavy view is pragmatic.

Science already accepts empirical relationships and instruments that are useful before their mechanisms are fully understood.

If an AI result:
- predicts reliably;
- replicates independently;
- supports intervention;
- and survives adversarial tests,

then demanding a human-readable internal mechanism may be unnecessary for some scientific tasks.

### Effect on our thesis
The Topic must not equate opacity with unreliability.

---

## Challenge 2 — Human science is not a clean baseline

Human researchers:
- hallucinate memories;
- miscite papers;
- p-hack;
- overfit theories;
- make coding mistakes;
- follow fashions;
- publish irreproducible work.

### Effect on our thesis
Do not compare AI against an imaginary flawless scientist.

The comparison should be:
**Which workflow produces more detectable, correctable and reproducible error?**

---

## Challenge 3 — Formal verification can create false confidence

A Lean proof can compile while:
- the formal statement differs from the intended theorem;
- assumptions encode away the interesting difficulty;
- a theorem is correct but trivial or already known;
- the formalization omits the scientific meaning users care about.

### Effect on our thesis
Formal verification is a strong correctness layer, not a universal truth machine.

---

## Challenge 4 — “AI narrows science” may reflect selection effects

Hao et al. report strong associations between AI adoption and collective narrowing.

Possible alternative explanations include:
- AI being adopted first in data-rich fields;
- career incentives selecting particular problems;
- publication and funding incentives predating AI;
- researchers choosing AI where existing benchmarks make progress easiest.

### Effect on our thesis
Use the study to motivate an ecosystem question, not a simple causal claim.

---

## Challenge 5 — Citation hallucination is measurable but narrow

Citation integrity is attractive because a reference can often be resolved objectively.

But a paper can have perfectly real citations and still be wrong.

### Effect on our thesis
Citation checking is one verification layer, not the definition of scientific reliability.

---

## Challenge 6 — More layers can create ceremonial verification

A complex pipeline can look rigorous while every checker depends on the same model family, same source corpus or same flawed benchmark.

### Effect on our thesis
Independence matters more than the number of agents.

A ten-agent pipeline can still be one epistemic system.

---

## Challenge 7 — Human accountability can become symbolic

A named researcher can technically “own” an AI-generated workflow without meaningfully understanding its outputs.

### Effect on our thesis
Accountability should require an actual correction path and defensible review, not only a name on the final artifact.

---

## Challenge 8 — Verification has costs and opportunity costs

Demanding experimental or formal validation for every generated candidate could erase much of the speed advantage AI offers.

### Effect on our thesis
The goal is risk-proportionate verification, not maximal verification everywhere.
