# Timeline

## 1960s — DENDRAL
Early AI systems use encoded chemical knowledge and search to infer molecular structures from mass-spectrometry data. Machine participation in scientific inference predates modern neural networks.

## 1980s–2000s — Machine discovery becomes a field
Systems such as BACON and later symbolic-regression approaches recover scientific relationships from data, shifting debate from “can a machine output a law?” to “what counts as discovery?”

## 2009 — Symbolic regression and natural laws
Work by Schmidt and Lipson demonstrates automated extraction of compact mathematical relationships from experimental data.

## 2020–2021 — AlphaFold 2
Protein-structure prediction reaches a level of practical accuracy that changes structural-biology workflows.

## 2023 — GNoME
Deep learning is used to expand the search for candidate stable materials at large scale.

## 2024 — Illusions of understanding
Messeri and Crockett formalize a broader epistemic concern: AI can increase productivity and apparent objectivity while producing scientific monocultures and illusions of understanding.

## 2025 — Hallucination becomes an infrastructure issue
Scientific discussion moves beyond anecdotal chatbot mistakes toward the burden of fabricated references, automated writing, review quality and verification.

## January 2026 — Individual gain, collective narrowing
Hao and colleagues publish a Nature analysis of 41.3 million papers associating AI use with large individual productivity and impact advantages alongside narrower collective scientific focus.

## April 2026 — Accuracy incentives and hallucination
Kalai and colleagues publish a Nature analysis arguing that common accuracy evaluations can incentivize guessing over abstention.

## April 2026 — Automated conjecture resolution
A preprint reports an end-to-end framework that resolves an open commutative-algebra problem and verifies the proof in Lean 4.

## May 2026 — Co-Scientist in Nature
Multi-agent hypothesis generation and refinement is published with biomedical experimental-validation cases.

## 2026 — Publisher policy becomes explicitly risk-based
Nature/Springer Nature formalizes a framework emphasizing human accountability, transparency, confidentiality and non-delegation of scholarly judgement.

## September 2026 — Papers become executable agents
Paper2Agent is published in Nature, turning papers, code, data and workflows into tested agent-accessible tools.

## September–October 2026 — Scientific slop becomes a benchmark target
Very recent preprints attempt to measure hallucinated references and global reasoning failures in AI-generated scientific papers.

## Current frontier
The frontier is moving from “Can AI generate scientific content?” to:
**Can verification, provenance and institutional quality control scale as fast as generation?**
