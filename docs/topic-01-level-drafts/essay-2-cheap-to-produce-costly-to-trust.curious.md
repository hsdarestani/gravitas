# Cheap to Produce, Costly to Trust

Two AI agents were each given a real, unpublished research project, six days and $3,000 of computing. They wrote the code and ran the experiments without help. The scientists who wrote the original papers then rejected both results [Kirgis, 2026](#ref-kirgis-2026).

## Making is cheap, judging is not

Producing text, code and whole experiments is getting cheap. Deciding whether the result is right, or worth doing, is not. The agents in this study did not fake results. They set a low bar for what counted as publishable and gave up too little on dead ends.

::: box "Diagram · where the effort moves (a concept, not data)"
Concept diagram. Producing text, code and experiments gets cheaper; checking whether they are right stays costly.
:::

## Checking is hard for everyone

Tests of "AI scientist" tools found invented results in the older open-source ones [Agrawal, 2026](#ref-agrawal-2026). A famous success, a paper accepted at a workshop, was one of three tries and was filtered by its developers [Lu, 2026](#ref-lu-2026). Human reviewers are not a gold standard either: two committees disagreed on about a quarter of decisions.

## A career gain and a narrower map

Scientists who use AI methods publish 3.02 times more and are cited 4.84 times more, but science as a whole studies a 4.63% narrower range of topics [Hao, 2026](#ref-hao-2026). Those are raw comparisons, and the gain shrinks when like is compared with like.

::: box "Chart 2 · Raw versus matched (Hao et al., R23)"
Horizontal bars. Raw advantage for scientists using AI: about plus 202 percent more papers and plus 384 percent more citations. Matched comparison at career year 10: plus 9.6 percent annual output and plus 24.5 percent annual citations.

The gap between a raw and a matched comparison is how much of the claim survives. The bars are not the same measure (our conversion of “×” to “+%”).
:::

## What to take from this

::: box "If you only remember"
1. Agents can do the engineering of research. The judgment is the unsolved part.
2. The strongest success stories come from developers and are selected.
3. What helps a career may narrow a field. The evidence is not yet causal.
:::

::: box "What you can do"
- Put a human check wherever one tool's output feeds another.
- Treat AI output as a hypothesis, not an authority.
- Keep practising the skills you hand over.
:::
