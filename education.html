01 / 08 The learning gap

A good explanation is not yet an experience.

Complex ideas become clearer when learners can change a condition and see what follows. But guidance and exploration rarely meet in one place.

01

Static teaching

A diagram can describe a system. It cannot let a learner test a different assumption.

02

AI answers alone

A fluent explanation does not show how the underlying model responds when a variable changes.

03

Unguided simulators

Controls and graphs invite exploration. Without the right questions, their meaning can stay hidden.

THE MISSING LINK≠

EXPLANATION ≠ EXPERIENCE

02 / 08 The learning loop

One question. One model. A deeper understanding.

Not a chatbot next to a diagram. Pulsar and the simulation are designed as two parts of the same learning journey.

01 / ASK↗

Ask.

Pulsar helps learners frame a question and notice the assumptions behind it.

02 / CHANGE↗

Experiment.

The learner changes variables in a model relevant to the subject.

03 / OBSERVE↗

Compare.

Different conditions reveal patterns, constraints and trade-offs.

04 / REASON

Explain.

Pulsar prompts learners to interpret what changed, and defend why.

STILL TO BE EVALUATED

Know the boundaries

  • Learning gains and course-specific assessment need learner studies
  • Models simplify reality; outputs are not empirical forecasts
  • Live answers depend on service availability and may be wrong
  • Institutional analytics and deployment are scoped per partner

A strong field. A specific contribution.

Simulation-based inquiry and guided tutoring already have strong examples. Our focus is an inspectable loop: a learner’s prediction, one controlled change, the resulting values and a conversation about that evidence in one place. This is our product direction, not a claim of exclusive capabilities or proven superiority.
