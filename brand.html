01

Foundations

The one paragraph everything else has to agree with.

Gravitas+ is a media project, an interactive lab and a community about how research actually gets made, in every field, and about the questions science keeps raising against itself.

The current centre of gravity is AI and machine learning in scientific research. Not which tool to use, but what happens to the idea of a hypothesis, of explanation, and of expertise when a machine is in the loop.

The Name

From the Latin gravitas: weight, seriousness, gravity. The visual identity takes the second meaning literally, as spacetime bending under a mass, and the whole system falls out of that one image: a G whose sides curve inward, a grid pulled into a well, a dark field with light in it. The plus is part of the name. It is never dropped, never replaced by the word "plus", and never set as ordinary text.

The Promise

Curiosity brings people in. Capability keeps them here.

This is the internal line, and it settles most arguments. It is why the site has a lab and not just a library, why a video opens a Topic instead of being the destination, and why a simulation you can break beats a diagram of the same idea.

What Every Piece Tries to Do

JobMeaningTest it has to pass
DeepenShow that something apparently simple has layers.A reader who already knew the topic learned something.
ClarifyMake something complicated understandable without talking down.A reader from another field followed it without a glossary.
ConnectLink science to philosophy, history, society and technology.The piece names a consequence outside its own discipline.
EngageMove people from watching to thinking, testing or arguing.There is something to do, break, dispute or vote on.

Who It Is For

  • The curious general reader, who wants the whole argument, not a teaser version of it.
  • Students and early researchers, who need method and vocabulary as much as conclusions.
  • Working researchers, in any field, who are downstream of the same arguments about evidence, explanation and automation.

One page serves all three. The depth switch re-renders an essay in place rather than sending people to a separate "for researchers" section, because splitting the audience at the door halves the value of every piece and makes people classify themselves before they know what is inside.

What Gravitas+ Is Not

  • Not a physics site. Physics is where the founder comes from and where examples are often clearest, but the claim is that a haematologist, a climate modeller and a labour economist are downstream of the same arguments. Say "computational science", not "computational physics", and reach outside the physical sciences on purpose.
  • Not a news feed. The unit is the question, not the announcement.
  • Not a personal channel. The host is credited on About and in channel art. Topic bylines, the hero and the footer are deliberately free of him, because the project is built to carry other contributors.
  • Not an authority that never moves. Corrections are logged publicly on the piece they belong to, not quietly edited away.
02

Personality and Archetype

A brand personality is only useful when it rules things out. This one does.

The Jungian Reading

Gravitas+ is a Sage brand with a Magician engine and a measured dose of Outlaw. The three are not equal partners: the Sage decides what is said, the Magician decides what is built, and the Outlaw sets the angle of attack.

Primary · 60%

The Sage

"The truth is worth the work."

The Sage's promise is understanding, and its fear is being fooled. That is precisely this project: evidence, demarcation, what peer review really catches, how a hypothesis earns its keep. It is also where the visual restraint comes from, since a Sage that shouts stops being believed. The Sage sets the register: patient, sourced, unhurried, and willing to say that a question is open.

Secondary · 30%

The Magician

"Here, run it yourself."

The Magician turns understanding into capability, which is the second half of the promise. It shows up as the lab, the simulations, the games, the code beside the claim, and the transformation the whole site is designed to cause: from watching to doing. A Magician without a Sage is hype. A Sage without a Magician is a lecture.

Tension · 10%

The Outlaw

"Try to break it, starting with ours."

Not rebellion for its own sake. This is the part that publishes the strongest case against our own reading, keeps a public correction log, and asks who pays and what that buys. It keeps the Sage honest and stops the whole thing sliding into institutional voice. Kept small on purpose: the target is the argument, never a person.

If Gravitas+ Were a Person

A researcher a decade into the work who has not lost the appetite. Explains at whatever depth you need without checking whether you are worth it, admits the parts nobody has settled, is visibly delighted when a result overturns something they said last year, and hands you the notebook instead of describing what is in it. Says "I do not know, but here is how we would find out" more often than any other sentence.

The Aaker Dimensions

Jung's archetypes say what story the brand is in. Jennifer Aaker's Dimensions of Brand Personality (1997) says what it is like to deal with, in five dimensions that can actually be measured: Sincerity, Excitement, Competence, Sophistication and Ruggedness, each rated 1 to 5 for how well it describes the brand. The two models answer different questions and both are worth having. Aaker's is the one to survey against, because a reader can score it.

Enduring brands lead on one dimension. Ours is Competence. Target below, with the facets that matter.

Competence primary 4.6

Reliable, intelligent, successful. The whole promise rests here: sources graded, code shipped beside the claim, limits stated, corrections logged. Read "successful" as authoritative rather than commercially large. This is the dimension a reader should feel within one paragraph.

Aaker, J. L. (1997). Dimensions of Brand Personality. Journal of Marketing Research, 34(3), 347 to 356. Scores are targets set by us, not survey results. If they are ever measured, the measured numbers replace these and the gap becomes the brief.

03

Voice and Tone

The voice never changes. The tone moves with the surface and with what the reader is doing at that moment.

The Voice, in Four Rules

  1. Lead with the claim, then defend it. The reader should know what is being argued by the end of the first sentence.
  2. Prefer the concrete. A number, a study, a mechanism or an example beats an adjective every time.
  3. Say the size of your confidence. "Measured", "estimated", "we think", "nobody knows" are all available, and picking the right one is the job.
  4. Address one reader. Not "many of you", not "the audience". One person, reading.

Honesty in the Interface

Copy is part of the product's integrity, so the interface says what is true about itself. Front-end-only features say so on screen. Content that does not exist yet keeps its card, loses its link and gains a Planned chip, because a citation that goes nowhere looks like scholarship and is not. The assistant answers from an index of what actually exists and says when it cannot match, rather than inventing a page.

Rewrites

Not this

"Scientists were stunned by this incredible discovery that changes everything."

Borrowed excitement, no claim, nothing to check.

This

"The result held up in three labs. What it means is still argued, and the argument is the interesting part."

States the evidence and names the open question.

Not this

"Let us break down the basics of machine learning in simple terms for beginners."

Lowers the ceiling before the reader has asked.

This

"A model can produce a sentence shaped like a hypothesis. Whether that is the same thing is the question."

Same accessibility, none of the condescension.

Not this

"Join our amazing community of curious minds and unlock exclusive content!"

Promotional register, and the promise is not ours to make.

This

"Come and argue with us. Members propose the next Topic and vote on it."

Says exactly what membership does.

Punctuation: No Em Dashes

There are none in shipped copy. Every dash was doing one of three jobs, and each job has a better mark.

JobUseExample
Aside or appositiveCommas or parenthesesno one, including its authors, can say why
ElaborationColonExplanation, evidence and demarcation: the arguments underneath every methods section
A hidden sentence boundaryFull stopYou may run as many tests as you like. The interesting question is which ones you choose

A readout with no value yetEn dash –Divergence time: –

Numbers, Units and Citations

  • Spell out one to nine in prose, use figures from 10 up, and always use figures with units.
  • Anything that updates in place (durations, counts, simulation readouts) is set with so digits do not jitter.
  • Reading time is stated before the commitment, not after it: "18 minutes for the essay, or 4 for the summary".
  • Sources are graded in three levels: start here, go further, primary. Every citation resolves to a real DOI, arXiv entry or publisher page.
  • Dates are written out (12 March 2026) in copy, and ISO in code.

Writing in Other Languages

The system is language-agnostic apart from two things. DM Sans does not cover every script, so a translated surface substitutes a matching sans with the same weight range rather than a default serif. And the punctuation rules above are English typographic rules: translations follow their own conventions, while keeping the ban on the dash-as-everything habit.

Logo

Two marks, one idea: something heavy is warping the space around it.

The Monogram

A bespoke capital G whose four sides pull inward into concave curves, giving a four-pointed silhouette with a squared counter. It reads at once as a G and as a gravity well distorting a square of space. This is the primary icon: profile pictures, favicons, app icons, the thumbnail watermark, the Ask Gravitas+ lockup and loading states.

The Logotype

The faceted G as the initial, then "ravitas" in a custom lowercase drawn to harmonise with it, closed by a superscript plus. Use it wherever the brand needs to be named: site header and footer, channel art, video intros, partnerships. It is not DM Sans and must never be rebuilt in a font.

On the Web, Use currentColor

A fixed fill breaks the light theme: a white logo on a cream page is nothing at all. Inline the SVG and set so the mark inherits and follows the theme. Give the mark one class in every place it appears, or the colour it sets on itself will override its container and paint dark ink on a dark fill.

Clear Space

Keep a margin equal to the cap height of the G around the logotype, and 25% of the icon width around the monogram. Nothing enters that zone: no text, no edges, no other logos, no busy imagery.

Minimum Sizes

  • Monogram: 24px on screen. 16px is acceptable for favicons only.
  • Logotype: 120px wide on screen, 25mm in print. Below that the plus and the lowercase detail break down, so switch to the monogram instead.
  • Reference sizes in production: header logotype 1.75rem tall, footer logotype 9rem wide, watermark and assistant mark 1.5rem.

The Mark Never Gives Way

In any row where space runs out, the logo is the last thing that may change and the first thing that is protected. It is never compressed, never scaled below its minimum, and never allowed to shrink so that something beside it can stay. The row sheds its own contents instead, in a stated order.

Everything left in the row is one height, taken from --gh-control: 2.5rem on a desktop, 2.75rem below 880px where it also has to clear a 44px touch target. That holds for the pills as well as the circles.

In the site header that order is: below 880px the navigation collapses into the menu and Sign in goes with it; below 400px Join Us follows, leaving the mark, the theme control and the menu. Both keep working from inside the menu, so nothing is merely hidden. The 880 figure is measured rather than chosen: logotype 149px, navigation 351px, actions 248px, two gaps and the gutters need 864px, so the full row is only offered where it fits.

Placing the Mark on Imagery

Space photography is busy. Prefer the white variant over a darker region, or add a radial darken (Royal Black at 30 to 50%) behind the mark, or reserve a calmer part of the composition. A logo does not need a container to read as one: do not put the mark in a bordered circle, which is avatar styling.

Misuse

Do

Use the supplied files at their own proportions, in one of the four brand colours.

Switch to the monogram when the logotype would fall below its minimum.

Give the mark room, and let the background do the contrast work.

Do not

Rebuild the wordmark in DM Sans or any other font, or drop the plus.

Stretch, condense, rotate, skew, outline, emboss or add a gradient or shadow to the mark. Glow behind a focal subject is fine. Effects on the mark are not.

Recolour outside the palette, reduce the opacity of a header or hero logo, or place the white mark on light imagery with no darkening.

Let a crowded row squeeze the mark. A logo that shrinks to make room for a button is a layout that has chosen the wrong thing to sacrifice.

06

Colour

Four fixed colours, one derived ramp, three signals. Depth comes from gradient, glow and space imagery, never from a new hue. Click any value to copy it.

The Four Primitives

Rough balance on a dark surface: about 70% Cosmos Blue or the ramp, 20% White Tint, 8% Sisal, 2% true black, gradient extremes and signals.

The Derived Accent

Sisal is the accent, and on a light ground it fails: at 1.4:1 against White Tint it is decoration pretending to be text. Rather than borrow a fifth hue, the light theme keeps the same warmth and takes it to ink weight. Bronze is Sisal with the value pulled down until it passes on paper, which is why it belongs in the palette as a derived value and not as a brand colour: nothing is ever specified as bronze, it is what --g-accent resolves to when the theme is light.

The Cosmos Ramp

Four colours cannot carry an interface. Cards, hovers, insets and menus all need to differ from the page without introducing a hue, so every surface value is Cosmos Blue mixed toward black or light: one hue, six depths.

Two Themes

Dark is the default outright, not "dark unless the operating system says otherwise".

The site has a look, and a visitor who has never touched the control should see the same site every time rather than one that depends on a setting they made for some other app. Only an explicit stored choice overrides it. Try the toggle in the header: this page is a live proof in both modes.

DM Mono, and Only Here

DM Mono is DM Sans's sibling rather than a second voice. It is scoped to the one place a single sans cannot do the job:

Display Type Is Measured Against Its Column

A hero headline wraps inside a column, not a window. Sizing it off vw put 84px type in a 558px track: about twelve characters a line, orphaned words, and it looked worst on the widest screens. Measure display type against its own container instead, with the viewport clamp kept as a fallback, and pair it with text-wrap: balance.

.hero__title {
  /* fallback for engines without container queries */
  font-size: clamp(2.6rem, 6.4vw, 4.5rem);
}
@supports (container-type: inline-size) {
  .hero__col { container-type: inline-size; }
  /* ~17 characters a line at every width */
  .hero__title { font-size: clamp(2.6rem, 10.8cqw, 4.5rem); }
}

Measure and Rhythm

  • Default measure token --g-measure is 68ch.
  • Article and prose bodies set at lead size use 62ch. A 68ch line at lead size runs to about 100 characters, past the point where the eye loses the return sweep.
  • Space between paragraphs must exceed space between lines (about 1.15em against a 1.72 line height), or the column reads as one undifferentiated mass.
  • Inside long-form, an h2 announces itself by position, with a short accent rule above it, rather than by more size.
  • Tracking: display -0.03em, headings -0.02em, body 0, uppercase labels 0.12 to 0.2em.

Layout Rules

  • One focal idea per section, and generous negative space around it. Resist filling every area: letting the darkness breathe is part of the identity.
  • Grids declare a column count that divides their item count. Six items as 3 plus 3, five as 3 plus 2. auto-fit packs as many as will fit and strands the remainder on a row of its own, which is where every orphaned card comes from.
  • Headings do not count their items. "Six Ways In" is fine as a display line, but a heading that says "five programmes" has to be edited every time the list changes.
  • Breakpoints in use: 520, 560, 600, 720, 760, 860, 900, 1000px. There is no grid framework, only the container, the stacks and the grid utilities.

Texture: The Spacetime Well

The signature graphic device. A coordinate grid curved by a mass, in two forms, always whispering.

The grid well is a square grid pulled into a funnel, and on the web it is one file, assets/spacetime-well.svg, drawn in White Tint strokes and inverted by CSS on the light theme. The contour well is a set of concentric rings distorted around a centre, used behind a face or the monogram. Both come from the same idea: space bending toward the subject.

.g-well--faint · 0.07
.g-well · 0.14 default
.g-well--strong · 0.2

Rules

  • Opacity stays between 7 and 20 percent, in Sisal or White Tint, over a Cosmos Blue or deep-space field. It carries depth, never decoration.
  • Centre it on the composition's focal point. .g-well--corner is the exception, for pages whose focal point is text at the left.
  • The class already carries a radial mask, so the grid fades out instead of ending at an edge. Do not remove the mask to get more of it: if you can read the grid, it is too strong.
  • Content always sits above it. The layer is pointer-events: none and aria-hidden.
  • Declare the image once, on :root. Declaring it inside one page's stylesheet is how identical panels ended up flat on some pages and textured on others.

The Star Field

.g-starfield is a set of tiny radial gradients at fixed positions, at 50% opacity, over the deep-space gradient. It belongs on heroes and join bands. It does not belong on small panels, where at that size the stars read as dead pixels rather than sky, and it is off entirely in the light theme.

Buttons

  • Every button is a pill, SemiBold, with a 2px lift and a glow on hover. Never a colour change outside the palette.
  • One primary per view. If two things look equally important, one of them is not.
  • A leading icon pushes the label right of centre. Correct it with .g-btn--lead, which shifts by about 45% of the icon and gap width: correcting it fully centres the label but leaves the ink block visibly left, which reads worse than the original problem.
  • In the header actions row a button does not size itself. Pill and circle alike take their height from --gh-control and centre the label in it, so Sign in, Join Us and the theme control are one height and cannot drift when the type scale or the button padding changes. A button's own padding sets its width, never the row's height.

All of them are set in DM Mono, uppercase, at .6 to .62rem. Three rules keep them consistent:

  • The line box is the type size. Inherit the body's 1.65 and the pill is drawn around the font's full ascent and descent, which an uppercase label uses neither end of: the caps ride high and the descender space shows underneath. Set line-height: 1 and let the padding do the centring, where it can be reasoned about. The pills carry .5em above and .48em below; the .02em is the caps' overshoot above the baseline.
  • Never a stretched flex item. A pill in a meta row inherits the row's height and lays its text out at the top. align-self: center, always.
  • Tracking is not centred by the box. Letter-spacing leaves a space after the last letter that the box counts and the eye does not, so the label reads left of centre. Half of it back with text-indent.

A status chip on a card is positioned rather than flowed, which brings two more: it is centred on a real line of text, the eyebrow on an entry or the meta line at the foot of a game or path card, never floating in a corner against nothing, and the line it shares keeps a gutter the width of the chip. An absolutely positioned chip takes no space, so without the gutter the text simply runs underneath it at the first narrow width.

Patterns Worth Inheriting

  • Depth switch. The rule must only ever hide the inactive side. Forcing display: block on the revealed side stamps on the element's own layout, and revert brings back the browser default rather than the author's rule. Hiding only also means the content is all visible with no JavaScript at all.
  • Panels and overlays. display is not animatable. Toggle opacity, transform and a delayed visibility, and set the transform origin to the control the panel came from, so it grows out of the button it belongs to.
  • One control per job. A launcher that becomes a close button while the panel shows its own close button makes the reader stop and choose.
  • Tickers. Triple the track so a copy always covers the viewport, expose only the first copy to assistive technology, and do not let it be paused or selected. A moving strip that reacts to a pointer invites people to grab it.
  • Sticky chrome. Measure the offsets, do not guess them.

Where the Section Marks Go

  • 1.25rem, bare. Rows in the mobile menu. Not the desktop nav: five words in a line with a mark in front of each is a toolbar, and this is a sentence of links.

The Geometry

  • Each glyph's ink centroid is solved onto (12, 12) in a 24-unit viewBox, by rasterising and iterating. The centroid of a stroked shape has no closed form in terms of its transform, because the stroke is laid on after the geometry. Bounding-box centring is the wrong quantity for an asymmetric glyph: it left one mark 1.27 units off its own centre.
  • Each is scaled to a common optical cap: 19.6 units for outline icons, 17.4 for solid ones, because a filled shape reads heavier than an outline of the same size. Two glyphs sit a few percent under the cap so no tip touches the frame.
  • Each group carries a counter-scaled stroke-width, so every icon still draws at 1.7 whatever its transform.
  • Those two rules together make the cap solve in one step rather than by iteration. Ink extent is s × (G + 1.7/s), where G is the longest side of the artwork with its stroke halo removed; the s cancels, and s = 17.9 / G. The six section marks were fitted this way, which is why their scales run from 0.962 to 1.178 and no two of their stroke attributes match.
  • The solid dot in the product set is re-cut to 3.1 units across, in every mark. At the radii they were drawn with, the dots landed anywhere between 2.5 and 4.0 units once each icon had been scaled to the cap, which read as six different dots rather than one motif. The rule is for the accent dot only: the filled head in the Join Us mark is structural, part of the figure, and keeps the size it was drawn at. Where growing a dot to 3.1 would close a gap the artwork was drawn with, the dot moves rather than shrinks, as it does in Sign in: the diameter is the rule, the clearance is the drawing.
  • All sixteen set shape-rendering="geometricPrecision". A residual of a third of a unit slides visibly as the page is zoomed, and it slides differently for each icon.
  • Render sizes are chosen so the leftover space divides to whole pixels at 100%: 1.25rem in a 2.25rem footer button, 1.125rem in a 2.5rem theme toggle.

Use the files as supplied. Do not re-transform, re-scale or swap in a stock equivalent, or the set will start disagreeing with itself.

Drawing a New Icon

  1. 24-unit viewBox, 1.7 stroke, round caps and joins, no fill unless the mark is inherently solid.
  2. Draw it, then measure the ink centroid and transform it onto (12, 12).
  3. Scale to the cap for its class, outline or solid, and counter-scale the stroke.
  4. Check it at 100% and 250% zoom, in both themes, against its neighbours in the same row.

Motion

Two kinds, with different jobs. Interface motion should be barely noticed. Brand motion is the one place the identity is allowed to perform.

Brand Motion: The Logo Sequence

The mark is not drawn. It is derived.

The animation's whole argument is that the logo is a consequence of the idea rather than a decoration on top of it. A coordinate field appears, a mass curves it, and the curvature that results is the concave side of the G. Anchor points land on the mark's real vertices, the outline draws along the real path data, and only then does the stroke hand over to a fill. Nothing in the sequence is a stand-in: if the path changes, the animation changes with it.

Sound is off until asked for, and it is synthesised rather than recorded. Open it on its own for a clean recording surface.

The Beats

BeatTimeWhat happens
The field0.0 to 1.2sA hyperbolic field draws outward from the centre. It is measured off the mark, not invented: along the mark's curve the quantity x² minus y² holds to within 5%, which makes the family hyperbolas sharing the diagonals as asymptotes.
Anchor points1.05 to 2.05sEleven outer vertices snap on in sequence, each one a real coordinate from the path.
Contour1.95 to 3.15sThe outline draws as a single stroke along the actual path length.
Counter form3.05 to 4.05sThe inner anchors and the counter arrive, which is the moment the G becomes legible as a letter.
Resolve4.0 to 4.75sStroke hands over to fill, a shockwave passes through the field, and the scaffolding retires while the mark is still centred.
Gravitas+4.85 to 6.1sThe camera pulls back and the mark turns out to have been the wordmark's G all along. The remaining glyphs arrive left to right, like writing.
Settle6.1 to 7.4sThe lock-up holds. Total runtime 7.4 seconds.

Rules for Using It

  • Use the full sequence where 7 seconds is affordable and the brand is the subject: a video intro, a conference open, a channel trailer. It is an overture, not a preloader.
  • For anything shorter, cut from Resolve, not from the middle. The construction reads as one gesture and half a construction reads as a glitch.
  • End on the lock-up. Never loop it: a mark that keeps reassembling itself stops looking inevitable and starts looking unfinished.
  • The field is White Tint on deep space, always. It is not restyled per video, and it does not run on the light theme.
  • Nothing else animates while it plays. One focal idea, in time as well as in space.

Sound

Every tone is synthesised at runtime rather than sampled, so the piece carries no licence and no audio file. A low drone fades in under the opening, anchor points ring as struck bells pitched from a D minor pentatonic, and the sequence ends on an open fifth left hanging rather than resolved. That choice is the brand in audio form: unresolved is the honest ending for a project about open questions.

Sound is off by default and never starts on its own. It exists for a screening or an export, not for a page a reader has just landed on.

Reduced Motion

The sequence is the one case where a shortened animation would be worse than none, so under prefers-reduced-motion it skips straight to the resolved lock-up, field included, at full opacity. The reader gets the end state rather than a fast version of the journey.

Rules

  • A theme flip transitions two properties only, background colour and text colour, at 260ms. Anything longer and the toggle feels laggy.
  • Motion carries meaning or it does not ship. A hover lift says "this is a door". A decorative animation says nothing and costs battery.
  • Reduced motion is handled twice. The site-wide clamp caps duration and iteration count, which is enough for a hover but wrong for anything whose end state matters: a ticker clamped to its end position parks the visible copy off screen. Any such component handles prefers-reduced-motion by name, turning the animation off and removing the duplicates.
  • Generative canvases must invalidate their frame cache on the theme event. A cached canvas that skips redraw when its geometry has not changed will keep painting white lines on a cream page until something unrelated dirties it.

Quality Bar

Every page ships against this list. It is a checklist, not an aspiration.

Accessibility

  • Exactly one h1 per page, headings in order, a skip link, lang set.
  • No unlabelled buttons, no missing alt, decorative layers marked aria-hidden.
  • Visible focus everywhere: 2px --g-focus at 3px offset, never removed.
  • 4.5:1 or better on every text pair, in both themes.
  • Reduced motion honoured, including the cases the global clamp does not cover.
  • Every interactive target reachable and operable by keyboard, in a sensible order.

Layout and Behaviour

  • No horizontal overflow at 360, 390, 768, 1024, 1440 and 1920px.
  • The logotype is at its full width at every one of them. If it is not, the header is carrying a control it should have handed to the menu.
  • Every control in the header actions row is the same height as its neighbours, pills included, in both themes.
  • No chip, badge or pill overprints text at any width.
  • The mobile menu opens, closes on selection, and navigates, on every page.
  • No console errors, no broken internal links, and no clickable dead links.
  • Nothing renders as an orphan row in a grid.
  • The page is readable and complete with JavaScript blocked.

Editorial

  • Casing follows the two registers. Punctuation carries no em dashes.
  • Every citation resolves to a real source.
  • Anything not yet built is marked Planned rather than linked.
  • Front-end-only features say so on screen.

Implementation

How to build a page that merges into this site instead of sitting beside it.

Files

  site.css       components this architecture needs, built on the system
  hero.css       home page hero only
  chat.css       Ask Gravitas+ launcher and panel
  site.js        header, menu, theme toggle, filters, depth switch
  hero.js        orbit, grid, meteors, comet

gravitas.css is the source of truth and should be edited, not re-derived. Anything it does not already provide goes in the shared second stylesheet, never into the system file. Design-system classes are prefixed g-. Page components are not.

Cascade Layers

@layer tokens, base, layout, components, utilities;

Later layers win, so a component style never gets out-fought by a stray utility or a base rule, and specificity stops being an argument.

Theme, Resolved Before First Paint

A theme applied after first paint means every light-mode visitor sees a flash of the dark site, which is the one bug a theme toggle cannot ship with. This goes inline in <head>, before any stylesheet, and it deliberately never reads prefers-color-scheme.

<script>
(function(){try{var s=localStorage.getItem('gravitas:theme');
document.documentElement.setAttribute('data-theme',s==='light'?'light':'dark');
}catch(e){document.documentElement.setAttribute('data-theme','dark');}})();
</script>

Flipping the attribute dispatches a gravitas:theme event. Anything painting to a canvas listens for it, re-reads the --g-canvas-* tokens and marks itself dirty.

Page Head

<title>Page · Gravitas+</title>
<link rel="icon" href="assets/favicon.svg" type="image/svg+xml">
<link rel="mask-icon" href="assets/monogram.svg" color="#003049">
<meta name="theme-color" content="#00263a">

The theme-color must not split on colour scheme. Dark is the default whatever the operating system says, and a scheme-split meta lets the browser chrome go cream on a dark page.

Skeleton

<body>
  <a class="g-skip-link" href="#main">Skip to content</a>
  <header class="g-header">…</header>
  <main id="main">
    <section class="g-section">
      <div class="g-container">…</div>
    </section>
  </main>

Naming

  • Block, element, modifier: .g-card, .g-card__title, .g-card--interactive.
  • Two unrelated components never share a class. The lab's canvases were .art, the same selector the article body uses for its prose column, which is a bug waiting for someone to write a rule for one of them.
  • Custom properties are prefixed --g-. Component-private ones take a single underscore, as in --_bg.
